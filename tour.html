PART 1 · EARLY HISTORY
Section 01

Flickers of Light Amidst Spiritual Darkness

Welcome to the beginning of the story of Jehovah's people in Thailand.

Jehovah’s name in early Bible translations

Long before the first publishers arrived in Thailand, Jehovah's name was already appearing in some early Thai Bible translations.

1841 Thai translation of Acts and 1884 Psalms

Key thoughtThis reminds us that Jehovah was preparing the way long before the first Witnesses arrived.

1898 · First known efforts to introduce Bible truth to Thailand

Horace Randle sent Bible literature and letters to missionaries throughout Asia, including Siam.

1931 · First known Witness visit

Claude Goodman became the first known Witness to visit Thailand.

1936 · Frank Dewar arrives

He arrived with only a few dollars in his pocket and a strong desire to serve Jehovah.

For a time, he was the only publisher in the country.

Point out the exhibitsNotice Frank's hat, dictionary, and even his bread recipe. These small items help us imagine the life of an early missionary who had to learn a new language, adapt to a new culture, and trust completely in Jehovah.

More publishers arrive

Others soon joined Frank, including Willy Unglaube, Ted Sewell, and Kurt Gruber.

Together they worked tirelessly despite language barriers and limited resources.

In the 1939 service year, they distributed thousands of literature.

Section 02

Working Diligently from Sunrise to Sunset

The need for a translator

For some years, there were no publications in Thai.

Brother Rutherford advised the brothers to keep working hard and to pray for a translator.

Within the same year, Jehovah answered their prayers!

They met Chomchai Inthaphan, who became one of the first local baptized Witnesses and Thailand's first dedicated translator.

Other local publishers came in the truth

Example: Kham-ai Chaiwan

He was a respected educator and church leader. After carefully examining the Scriptures, he embraced the truth even though it cost him his position and financial security.

He willingly sacrificed those things because he had found something more valuable.
